How the Two Designs Compare

Six design choices, and what each one does to the person using the page.

🔤 Typography

GoodOne clean font family with a clear size scale.

BadFive unrelated fonts, from giant to tiny.

Effect on the userConsistent type feels trustworthy and is easy to scan. Mixed type makes the eyes re-adjust on every line.

🎨 Color Consistency

GoodDark navy, white and one blue accent.

BadRed, neon green, yellow and purple fighting each other.

Effect on the userA small palette guides attention to what matters. Clashing colors tire the eyes and hide the important actions.

📐 Spacing and Alignment

GoodSame margins and padding everywhere, all content on one grid.

BadEvery section has different spacing and drifts left or right.

Effect on the userEven spacing groups related things and creates calm rhythm. Random spacing feels sloppy and is harder to follow.

🏷️ Visual Hierarchy

GoodBig headline, medium section titles, smaller body text.

BadTiny headings next to huge paragraphs, so nothing stands out.

Effect on the userPeople instantly see what to read first. Without hierarchy they must hunt for the main message.

👓 Readability

GoodDark text on light backgrounds, comfortable line spacing.

BadLow contrast, cramped lines and awkward wrapping.

Effect on the userReadable text takes less effort. Poor contrast tires everyone and shuts out people with low vision.

🧩 Consistency

GoodEvery button, card and heading follows the same rules.

BadEvery button has a different shape, size and color.

Effect on the userUsers learn once and can predict the rest. Inconsistency makes them hesitate and ask "is this clickable?"

What Makes the Good Version Effective

A quick checklist you can apply to any website you build.

  1. One font family. Every line looks like part of the same page.
  2. A limited color palette. Navy, white and one blue accent point to the buttons.
  3. Clear hierarchy. Headline, section titles and body text each have their own size.
  4. Consistent spacing. Every section uses the same padding and cards share a grid.
  5. Readable text. Strong contrast and relaxed line spacing.
  6. Consistent components. All buttons and cards look and behave alike.
“Good design is not about adding more decoration. It is about presenting the right information clearly, consistently, and beautifully.”
